About this exhibit

Sources & conventions Gustav Mie (1908), Beiträge zur Optik trüber Medien, speziell kolloidaler Metallösungen. Annalen der Physik 25, 377–445. Figures 3–10: pp. 407–408; Figure 14: p. 423. Hans Blumer (1925), Radiation diagrams of small dielectric spheres. Zeitschrift für Physik 32, 119–134. DOI: 10.1007/BF01331653. Figures from the supplied translation by Robin Klupp Taylor (2025).

The original illustrations come from the supplied papers or the linked open scans. Colour overlays use the laboratory’s harmonics, Mie coefficients and coupled-sphere solver. Settings and sliders stay inside the frame. Click a spectrum to inspect its settings, then use the lesson button to explore it in the laboratory.

Mie's multipoles use the laboratory's normalized, angular vector harmonics on the unit sphere. Phase controls the instantaneous field arrows; colour shows time-independent |F|². The original drawing does not specify an absolute time origin.

Blumer's Paper scale view shows i₁ = |S₁|² and i₂ = |S₂|² separately on each figure's printed, absolute linear radial scale. Coloured polarization-resolved 3D surfaces and their meridional contours share that scale. There is no peak normalization or logarithm in this view. His γ = 180° is the app's forward θ = 0°. The strongest forward lobes in Figures 3 and 6 continue beyond the original image and are clipped at its edge. The 3D view shows linearly polarized light with E along x, as in the linked lesson 03 setting. Paper scale restores the aligned absolute comparison.

Mie's Figure 14 has nine app-generated scattering spectra for diameters 20–180 nm, at volume fraction 10⁻⁶ (1 ppm), with electric dipole a₁ alone. They use his historical optical constants and wavelengths in water, interpolated over 420–650 nm. Selecting a curve updates Paper settings. The separate lesson 05 button transfers that diameter, wavelength, dispersive optical constants and scattering-per-millimetre ordinate together. Historical rounding, approximations, hand interpolation and scan geometry leave visible differences. The app curves are not fitted to the scanned lines.

Rayleigh’s two 1871 figures compare relative scattering and transmission. His thickness ratios do not specify a particle size or an absolute concentration: the illustrative microscopic settings and relative optical-depth normalization are stated explicitly.

Oldenburg et al. (1998): Figure 1a checks the stated 60 nm core radius and 20, 10, 7 and 5 nm shells with a single shared ordinate scale. With Thesis Au, all four spectra reproduce the scan to about 1.6% RMS, without fitting dimensions or wavelengths.

In Figures 3a and 3b, Paper sizes uses the caption dimensions and separate amplitude registration. Fit experiment selects effective core and uniform-shell dimensions fitted to the solid completed-shell spectra, with one positive amplitude multiplier per curve. The material remains Johnson–Christy gold with the documented A = 1 thin-shell correction; core n = 1.45 and medium n = 1.33. There is no offset or wavelength shift. The fitted settings, caption dimensions and TEM shell thickness are shown together and transfer to lesson 05.

These optical fits do not recover unique structural dimensions. Different curves from the same nominal population favour different effective core sizes. They substantially improve the caption settings entered into this app, but do not uniformly beat the original dotted curves: the thinnest 120 nm example remains better described by the published simulation. The thesis confirms DLS size overestimation and quantitative TEM sizing (pp. 26–27); a smaller-core correction does not improve these spectral fits. Roughness, incomplete shells and size distributions remain possible sources of mismatch. Early gold-island growth is outside the uniform concentric-shell model. CSV retains unscaled cross sections and the actual dimensions of every app curve.

Targets shows the original digitized solid experimental points as open circles for the selected fit. Curve labels use experimental TEM shell thicknesses; the caption’s theoretical thickness is retained separately in Paper settings. Figure 3a’s maxima are reported separately: the top experimental curve (33 nm TEM shell) peaks near 731 nm, while its single-size fit peaks at 753 nm. The whole-spectrum RMS does not establish agreement with the experimental maximum. The single-size fits are unchanged from version 10.

The comparison selector also offers Size spread. This fits independent Gaussian number distributions in core radius and shell thickness, with one common core population per panel. Absolute cross sections are averaged and the size-dependent gold damping is recalculated for each shell. The displayed SDs describe spread between particles, not uncertainty on the means. The distributions improve the spectral fits, but their widths are effective optical parameters and may absorb roughness or other model limitations. The top Figure 3a maximum is 744 nm in this model, compared with the experimental mark near 731 nm. The separate lesson 05 button compares the mean single particle; that lesson does not reproduce the population-averaged spectrum.

What the Oldenburg thesis check found

Thesis Au uses the documented thin-shell correction to the bulk Johnson–Christy dielectric function (1999 thesis, pp. 15–19). The electron collision frequency is γbulk + vF/t with A = 1. Bulk Au provides a comparison. The correction broadens the lines but does not remove every discrepancy with the 1998 figure. The exact optical input files and size-distribution parameters for that figure are not provided.

The app’s coating calculation was independently checked by solving the four boundary equations with separate Bessel-function routines: 84 cases agreed to about 10⁻⁹ in the tested coefficients and efficiencies. The thesis also reports tests against MIEV0 and MIEVcoat (p. 24). The remaining mismatch does not establish a bug in the historical extinction code.

One methodological issue appears in the thesis’s description of the polydisperse angular routine (p. 91): complex scattering amplitudes are averaged before squaring. For an incoherent suspension of separate particles, intensities should be averaged. The same passage describes averaging extinction cross sections separately, so this issue alone does not explain Figure 3. The original program bodies and figure-generation inputs would be needed for a definitive code audit.

Zhang et al. (2013), Figures 1b and 1c, compare gold dimers under parallel and perpendicular polarization. The app curves retain the absolute absorption-power scale in µW per sphere, with the stated incident intensity of 1 mW/µm². The same sphere sizes, gaps, polarization and Johnson–Christy optical data transfer to lesson 08.

Earlier core–shell papers with useful figures

Scharfman (1954), Scattering from Dielectric Coated Spheres in the Region of the First Resonance is an early follow-up; its figure contents have not been verified here.

Morriss & Collins (1964), Optical Properties of Multilayer Colloids compares measured and calculated spectra of silver-coated gold colloids.

Pilat (1967), Optical Efficiency Factors for Concentric Spheres has five figures, including efficiency factors versus size and shell thickness. Open author copy ↗
