Calculation, coordinates and limits

The two curves use the same particles, pigment loadings, binder and film thickness. They differ only in the Lambertian substrate reflectance, R = 0.90 or R = 0.03. Both are calculated for every card design. Reflectance is a spectral property of this model film and geometry. Changing the light source changes its colour coordinates, rather than its spectrum.

The default solves scalar radiative transfer by discrete ordinates and adding–doubling, with 24 ordinates per hemisphere and the full Mie angular phase functions. It includes TiO₂, repeated scattering, substrate returns and Fresnel reflection inside the binder. At 45° / 0°, illumination is circumferential and surface gloss is excluded. The d / 8° options use ideal diffuse illumination and viewing at 8° to the surface normal. SCI includes the direct Fresnel surface reflection. SCE removes it. Actual instrument ports and rough-surface gloss are not simulated.

The Kubelka–Munk comparison uses K = 2μa and S = ¾μs(1 − g), derived from the Mie absorption, scattering and scattering-weighted asymmetry. It solves the finite layer over each substrate and applies a diffuse Saunderson surface correction. It predicts diffuse hemispherical reflection, rather than an angle-resolved measurement. Its geometry is therefore fixed.

CIEXYZ, xyY, CIELAB, CIELCh and CIELUV use the selected source white and observer, with Y = 100 for a perfect reflecting diffuser. C* is chroma and h° is hue angle. The visible interval is 380–780 nm with interpolation at 1 nm. The plotted optical calculation uses 10 nm spacing. Optional near-infrared data use 20 nm spacing to 1700 nm, stopping at the common validity limit of the constituent datasets. Near-infrared values never enter the colour coordinates. The binder is an ideal clear material with a constant real index, including in the near-infrared.

The swatches are approximate sRGB previews with Bradford adaptation to D65. Native CIE coordinates are not white-balanced to a different source. Screen gamut and the use of a 10° observer can limit the preview. Dense pigment interactions, aggregation, fluorescence, surface roughness and wave interference between particles are omitted. Concentrated films remain qualitative predictions.

About swatches, saved settings and backups

Each colour pair uses the saved particles, loading and light source. Transmission includes forward diffuse light. Reflection is viewed along the normal under normal incidence, with clear-surface glare omitted. The preview uses a flat reference path through the sample, rather than a container render. Its length is the vial diameter, cuvette gap, paint-film thickness or representative glass thickness. Films are shown without a test-card backing. Paint TiO₂, transparent glass scatterers and glass-matrix absorption are included. The swatch tooltip gives the path length.

Hue follows the displayed swatch colour, starting at red. Near-neutral colours are placed last. Particle size is the outer mean diameter, including the shell. Material sorting keeps alloys and core–shell particles in separate groups.

Favourites keep the complete particle, concentration, pigment loadings and scene settings. Restoring a saved scene affects all samples in the Lightbox. Untick the option above to use the current scene instead. Favourites and the selected sorting order remain stored in this browser between sessions.
